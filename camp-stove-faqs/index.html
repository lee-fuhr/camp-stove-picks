Camp stove questions answered

Updated June 2026Independently researchedNo paid placement.

Quick answer

The best camp stove for you depends on how you cook, how many people you feed, and where you're going. Canister stoves are the easiest for most car-campers and backpackers, while liquid-fuel stoves handle cold weather and remote trips better, and budget-friendly propane tabletop stoves are perfect for drive-in camping.

What type of camp stove should you buy?

The three main types of camp stoves are canister stoves, liquid-fuel stoves, and propane tabletop stoves. Canister stoves screw onto a small, disposable fuel canister and are the most popular choice for car camping and backpacking because they light instantly and simmer well. Liquid-fuel stoves burn white gas or kerosene and are the most reliable in cold temperatures and at high altitudes. They require a bit more maintenance and priming, but they’re the go-to for serious backcountry trips. Propane tabletop stoves are heavy but incredibly stable and are a favorite for car campers who cook elaborate meals. Each type has trade-offs, so think about your typical camping style before buying.

How do you choose between propane and isobutane fuel?

Propane is common for large, two-burner tabletop stoves and works well in warm weather, but it doesn’t vaporize as well in cold temperatures. Isobutane (often blended with propane) is used in backpacking canisters and performs moderately better in the cold, though it still struggles below freezing. If you camp in winter or at altitude, a liquid-fuel stove or a specially formulated cold-weather isobutane canister is a smarter bet. For three-season car camping, standard propane or isobutane will do just fine. The key is matching your fuel to the conditions you’ll actually face.

What size stove do you need for your group?

For solo or two-person trips, a compact single-burner canister stove is plenty. These stoves weigh very little and boil water in just a few minutes. For groups of three or four, a mid-size two-burner propane stove gives you the versatility to cook a main dish and a side at the same time. Larger groups of five or more might appreciate a bigger dual-burner stove or even a separate single-burner backup. If you’re car camping with a big crew, a full-size propane stove with a sturdy base is the most convenient. Remember that bigger stoves also mean larger fuel canisters, so consider how much space you have in your vehicle.

How do you maintain a camp stove to make it last?

After each trip, wipe down the burner heads and any food debris from the stove body. For canister stoves, keep the rubber O-ring on the connection clean and lightly lubed with silicone grease if it starts to dry out. Liquid-fuel stoves need periodic cleaning of the jet and generator, a small brush and the manual’s instructions are your friends. Store your stove in a dry place, and if it’s a liquid-fuel model, empty or cap the fuel bottle properly. Stoves that sit with leftover fuel can develop clogs. Most modern stoves are built to last many seasons with basic care, but a neglected stove will let you down when you need it most.

What safety precautions matter most when using a camp stove?

Never use a camp stove inside a tent, vehicle, or enclosed space, even with the door open, carbon monoxide can build up to deadly levels. Always set the stove on a stable, level, non-flammable surface away from dry grass and overhanging branches. Keep flammables like fuel bottles, paper towels, and loose clothing at least a few feet away. Before lighting, check the connections for leaks by listening for a hiss or applying a bit of soapy water. And always carry a small fire extinguisher or a large pot of water nearby, especially during dry season. It’s rare that something goes wrong, but being prepared is simple and could save your trip.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use a camp stove indoors?

No, never use a camp stove inside a tent, cabin, vehicle, or any enclosed space. They produce carbon monoxide, which is odorless and deadly. Even with ventilation, the risk is too high.

How do I light a camp stove if my igniter breaks?

A broken piezoelectric igniter is common. Carry a dedicated spark lighter, a ferro rod, or a disposable lighter as a backup. Always light the match or lighter before turning on the gas to avoid a burst of flame.

What's the best fuel for cold-weather camping?

For temperatures below freezing, standard isobutane canisters become sluggish. Use a winter-specific isobutane blend or switch to a liquid-fuel stove that burns white gas, which vaporizes reliably in very cold conditions.

Are camp stoves allowed during fire bans?

In most jurisdictions, stoves with an on/off switch and closed fuel source are allowed even during burn bans, because they don’t produce embers. But you must check local regulations, some areas prohibit all open flames, including stoves, during extreme dry conditions.

How do I know if my fuel canister is empty?

Weigh the canister, empty ones are significantly lighter. Alternatively, try floating it in water: an empty canister will float, while a full one sinks. Never shake it near a flame to test it.

Can I refill disposable isobutane canisters?

Manufacturers do not recommend it, and it’s risky. The valve system isn’t designed for repeated refilling, and improper handling can cause leaks or explosions. Stick to using canisters as intended and recycle them responsibly.

In shortPicking the right camp stove is about matching your cooking style to the conditions you’ll encounter. A well-chosen stove, paired with good fuel and a few safety habits, will make your outdoor meals simple and satisfying. Take your time, read reviews from other campers, and you’ll find a stove that serves you reliably for years.
